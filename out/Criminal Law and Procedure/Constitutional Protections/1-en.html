The Constitution guarantees the right to counsel in two places—the Fifth Amendment and the Sixth Amendment. The Sixth Amendment right to counsel:

• automatically attaches once formal judicial proceedings commence (e.g., indictment, filing of formal charges, preliminary hearing, arraignment) and

• guarantees that criminal defendants will thereafter have the assistance of counsel during all critical stages of prosecution (i.e., any event where the absence of counsel may prejudice the defendant's right to a fair trial).

Here, the suspect was questioned by the detective before the suspect had been indicted for robbery, so formal judicial proceedings had not yet commenced. Therefore, the Sixth Amendment right to counsel had not attached when the suspect made the incriminating statement (Choices A and C).

In contrast, the Fifth Amendment right to counsel applies when a suspect is subjected to a custodial interrogation before judicial proceedings commence. However, a suspect must invoke this right by making a specific, unambiguous statement requesting counsel. A suspect who received and understood the Miranda warnings but did not invoke his Miranda rights waives the right to remain silent by making an uncoerced statement to the police—and that statement is admissible at trial.

(Choice D) Here, the suspect received and understood his Miranda warnings, but the suspect never made a specific and unambiguous request for counsel. And because there is no indication that the suspect's incriminating statement was coerced, the suspect waived the right to remain silent by voluntarily making that statement. Accordingly, the suspect's statement is admissible at trial.

(Choice B) The detective was not required to inform the suspect that his attorney was on the way, nor was the detective required to inform the attorney that the suspect was being questioned.

Educational objective:
The Sixth Amendment right to counsel automatically attaches once formal judicial proceedings commence.
In contrast, the Fifth Amendment right to counsel applies whenever a suspect is subjected to a custodial interrogation, and a suspect must invoke this right by making a specific, unambiguous statement requesting counsel.

References

Moran v. Burbine, 475 U.S. 412, 421–422 (1986) (holding that police are under no obligation to inform a suspect that an attorney has been trying to reach him and may even withhold that information intentionally, so long as the Sixth Amendment right to counsel has not yet attached).
