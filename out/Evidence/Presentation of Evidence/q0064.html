Character for truthfulness Reputation or opinion testimony Extrinsic evidence

SIC = Specific instance of conduct.

A witness can be impeached弹劾 (Impeachment): 对证人在法庭上作证的信誉度提出质疑，以削弱其证言的可信度。 (ie, discredited) with evidence of self-interest or bias偏见与利害关系 (Bias/Self-interest): 证人对案件结果有利害关系或与一方当事人有特定关系，可能促使其作伪证。—ie, something that may motivate the witness to testify falsely. Evidence of bias can be introduced intrinsically through the witness's own testimony OR extrinsically from any other source—including another witness's testimony.* Since a prosecutor's promise to drop pending charges may motivate a witness to testify falsely, evidence of the prosecutor's promise is admissible for impeachment purposes.

*The Federal Rules of Evidence do not require that a party lay a foundation by asking the witness about an alleged bias before introducing extrinsic evidence of that bias, but many states' rules of evidence do.

(Choice A) Hearsay传闻证据 (Hearsay): 庭外陈述，在法庭上作为证据提出以证明所主张事项的真实性，通常不可采。 is an out-of-court statement offered to prove the truth of the matter asserted therein. Since the prosecutor's statement is being offered to show that the witness's testimony is motivated by self-interest—not to prove that the charges would actually be dropped if the witness testified favorably—it is not hearsay.

(Choice B) The law does encourage plea-bargaining辩诉交易 (Plea-bargaining): 刑事案件中被告人以承认有罪换取公诉人减刑或撤销部分指控的协议。—ie, a criminal defendant's agreement to plead guilty or no contest to a crime (not seen here) in exchange for some form of consideration from the prosecutor (eg, probation in lieu of jail time). But this does not provide a basis for excluding evidence.

(Choice C) Statements made by and offered against a party-opponent诉讼对方 (Party-opponent): 诉讼中处于对立面的一方当事人，对方当事人的承认不属于传闻证据。 are not hearsay. This includes statements made by a party's agent on a matter within the scope of the agency relationship. Here, there is no evidence that the witness was the prosecutor's agent, so this is not a basis to admit the testimony.

📌 Educational Objective

Evidence that a witness's testimony was motivated by self-interest or bias is admissible to impeach the witness—ie, to discredit the truthfulness or reliability of the witness's testimony.
