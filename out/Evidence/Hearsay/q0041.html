Statement against interest
(FRE 804(b)(3))
Hearsay statement that reasonable person in unavailable declarant's position would only make if true because it:
  • is contrary to declarant's proprietary or pecuniary interest
  • tends to invalidate declarant's claim against someone else or
  • exposes declarant to civil or criminal liability
FRE = Federal Rule of Evidence.

The rule against hearsay generally bars the admission of an out-of-court statement offered to prove the truth of the matter asserted therein. But there is an exception for a statement against interest—ie, a statement that a reasonable person in the declarant's position would only have made if he/she believed it to be true because it:

  • is contrary to the declarant's proprietary or pecuniary interest
  • tends to invalidate the declarant's claim against someone else or
  • exposes the declarant to civil or criminal liability.

For this exception to apply, however, the declarant must be unavailableDeclarant Unavailable / 证人无法出庭A requirement under FRE 804 (e.g., due to death, privilege, illness, or refusal to testify) for certain hearsay exceptions to apply. as a witness (eg, deceased).

Here, the friend's out-of-court statement ("I was paid to set the fire") is hearsayHearsay / 传闻证据An out-of-court statement offered in evidence to prove the truth of the matter asserted. since it is being offered for its truth. But a reasonable person in the friend's position would only have said this if it were true since the statement exposed the friend to criminal liability for arsonArson / 纵火罪The criminal act of deliberately setting fire to property.. And since the friend is unavailable to testify (deceased), the wife's testimony as to the friend's statement is admissible under the statement against interest hearsay exception.

📌 Educational Objective

A statement against interest is a hearsay exception for statements that are so contrary to the declarant's proprietary, pecuniary, or penal interest that a reasonable person in the declarant's position would only have said it if he/she believed it to be true. But this exception only applies if the declarant is unavailable as a witness.
